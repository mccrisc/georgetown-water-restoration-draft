Flood Cleanup in Georgetown, TX

Extraction and cleanup after storm water, appliance overflows, and supply-line floods — with drying that targets the materials that stay wet after the floor looks dry.

What “flood cleanup” means here

Flood cleanup in Georgetown covers more than mopping. Standing water has to leave the structure; wet insulation, pad, and wall cavities often need controlled drying; and category of water (clean supply vs. contaminated) changes how carefully contents and surfaces are handled.

Our extraction & cleanup sequence

  1. Assess & classify Identify the source, affected rooms, and whether water may include sewage or outdoor contaminants. Safety first.
  2. Extract standing water Truck-mounted or portable extraction to remove bulk water from floors and carpets.
  3. Remove unsalvageable materials when needed Saturated pad, severely damaged drywall, or contaminated soft goods may need removal — we explain options before we cut.
  4. Set drying equipment Air movers and dehumidifiers aimed at measured wet zones, not guesswork alone.
  5. Monitor & document Moisture readings and photos support your records. Claim decisions remain with your insurer.

Storm water vs. indoor overflows

Central Texas storms can drive water under doors, through weep holes, or into low spots near the San Gabriel River corridor and neighborhood drainage. Indoor failures — water heaters, supply lines, dishwashers — often look smaller but soak the same cavities. Both need prompt flood cleanup attention.

Sewage or gray water? Treat it as contaminated. Limit contact, ventilate if safe, and call for professional cleanup rather than DIY scrubbing that spreads material.
