What to Do After a Flood (First 24 Hours)

Homeowners in Georgetown and across Williamson County often face flash flooding after heavy rain, appliance overflows, or storm drainage backups. The first day sets the tone for how much secondary damage you face.

Life-safety first. If anyone is hurt, you smell gas, or water is near live electrical panels you cannot safely shut off, call 911 or your utility. This guide is general mitigation advice — not emergency-services or medical guidance.

Hour 0–1: stabilize

  1. Get people and pets to dry ground. Avoid wading near outlets, extension cords, or appliances still plugged in.
  2. Stop the source if it is safe. Shut the fixture valve or the main water shutoff. Kill power to the affected circuit only if you can reach the breaker dry-footed.
  3. Call for help when the event is bigger than towels. Dial (512) 555-0147 for emergency water damage response in Georgetown, or use our emergency page checklist while you wait.

Hours 1–6: document, then limit spread

  • Photograph every affected room — wide shots and close-ups of water lines on walls.
  • Note the approximate start time and whether the water looked clean, dirty, or sewage-related.
  • Move unsoaked valuables up and off wet floors if it is safe.
  • Do not run HVAC blindly through saturated ducts without guidance.

Hours 6–24: extraction and drying plan

Standing water and soaked carpet need extraction equipment — not just fans pointed at a puddle. Our flood cleanup work focuses on removing water, mapping moisture, and setting drying gear so materials can dry from the inside out.

In Georgetown’s warm months, moisture left in place raises the risk of secondary damage. See our mold clock guide for the 24–48 hour moisture window (general risk notes only).

First-day checklist

  • ☐ People safe; 911 called if needed
  • ☐ Source controlled or help en route
  • ☐ Photos and time notes started
  • ☐ Valuables moved off wet floors (if safe)
  • ☐ Mitigation crew or DIY plan for extraction
  • ☐ Insurance documentation started (see documentation guide)

FAQ

Can I wait overnight before calling?

If water is still spreading, or if carpet, pad, or wall cavities are soaked, waiting usually makes the job larger. Small, contained clean spills are different — use judgment, or call us and describe the scene.

Is flood water always “clean”?

Storm water, drain backups, and sewage can introduce contaminants. When in doubt, treat the water as potentially unsafe and keep kids and pets away until a professional assesses it.

Will insurance cover everything?

Coverage depends on your policy and cause of loss. We help with mitigation documentation; we do not guarantee claim approval. More in our insurance documentation guide.
