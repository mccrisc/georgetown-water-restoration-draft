Insurance Documentation After Water Damage

After water damage in a Georgetown home, clear records help you talk with your insurer and with any restoration crew. This guide covers practical documentation. It does not promise that a claim will be approved, paid, or covered — that depends on your policy and insurer.

No claim-approval promises. We help with mitigation documentation (photos, moisture notes, scope of work performed). Whether a claim is approved is between you and your insurer.

Start before heavy cleanup

Once materials are tossed or walls are opened, it is harder to show what the loss looked like. If it is safe, photograph first — then extract and dry. See what to do after a flood for the first-day sequence.

Photo checklist

  • Wide shots of each affected room from two angles
  • Close-ups of water lines on walls, baseboards, and ceilings
  • The suspected source (burst fitting, appliance, roof leak area)
  • Serial plates on damaged appliances if visible and safe
  • Contents that were wet (furniture legs, boxes, rugs)
  • Timestamps enabled on your phone camera when possible

Notes worth keeping

  1. When it started — date/time you first noticed water, and when it stopped.
  2. What you did — shutoffs, who you called, temporary moves of belongings.
  3. Water type clues — clean supply line vs. dirty storm water vs. possible sewage (describe; don’t guess chemically).
  4. Who entered the property — mitigation crew, plumber, adjuster visits.

How we support documentation

FAQ

Should I file a claim before calling a restoration company?

Policies and preferences differ. Many homeowners call for emergency mitigation first so damage does not grow, then open or continue a claim. Ask your insurer about emergency mitigation allowances in your policy.

Do you bill insurance directly?

Billing arrangements vary by job and carrier. We will explain options clearly before work expands beyond emergency stabilization. Never assume direct billing is automatic.

What if my claim is denied?

That decision belongs to the insurer. We still document the mitigation we performed. For policy disputes, speak with your carrier or a qualified advisor — we are not your attorney or public adjuster unless separately engaged as such (we are not).
