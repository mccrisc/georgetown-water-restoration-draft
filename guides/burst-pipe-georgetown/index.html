Burst Pipe & Winter Freeze Tips for Georgetown Homeowners

Georgetown winters are milder than North Texas, but hard freezes still hit Williamson County. Supply lines in garages, attic spaces, and exterior walls can freeze and split — and a burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons before anyone notices.

Active spray or ceiling bulge? Shut the main water valve if you can do so safely, keep clear of sagging ceilings, and call (512) 555-0147 for emergency water damage help. Call 911 if structural collapse or electrical contact is a risk.

Why burst pipes show up here

Fast growth around Georgetown means a mix of new builds and older plumbing. Freeze events, aging supply lines, failed ice-maker lines, and water-heater or washer hose failures are common local call drivers — not just “flood season” storms.

If a pipe has already burst

  1. Main shutoff — usually near the meter or where the line enters the house. Know this location before winter.
  2. Power caution — if water reached outlets or the panel area, stay dry-footed; call a professional or 911 if unsafe.
  3. Call mitigation — extraction and drying limit how far water travels into flooring and walls. Start at our emergency water damage page if you need the first-hour checklist.
  4. Plumber + restoration — a plumber stops and repairs the line; restoration handles the water that already escaped. You often need both.

Before a freeze (Georgetown / Williamson)

  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses; insulate exposed sillcocks.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during hard freezes.
  • Let a pencil-thin stream run from far fixtures if a deep freeze is forecast and you will be away.
  • Know your main shutoff and share it with anyone house-sitting.

After the water stops

Even a short burst can soak pad, subfloor, and lower drywall. Follow the same moisture clock as other water losses — see the mold clock guide — and keep insurance photos before major tear-out.

FAQ

My pipe burst while I was at work — is it too late?

Call anyway. Stopping ongoing flow and starting extraction still reduces how much material must be replaced. Describe how long water may have run so crews can plan.

Do you repair the pipe itself?

We focus on water extraction, drying, and mitigation. Pipe repair is typically a plumber’s scope; we coordinate timing so drying is not delayed.
